Three things to know before anything else

1. A finished sale is never lost. If the printer jams, the internet drops or the lights go out after you press Complete sale, the sale is already saved. Only the printing can fail, never the record.

2. There is no "forgot my password" in Sukoon. Nobody — not even the person who built it — can let you back in. That is why Section 4 makes you create a second owner account, and why that step is not optional.

3. One printed page protects everything. The recovery sheet in Section 13 is the only key to your off-site backups. Print it, take it home, and never photograph it.

1What Sukoon is

One program that does the job of the register, the khata book and the stock diary — and never forgets, never adds up wrong, and never loses a page.

Sukoon runs on one computer in your shop. That computer holds everything: every product, every sale, every customer's balance. The other counters simply look at the same information over the shop's own network.

What it does for you

Rings up sales

Scan or type items, take cash, card or put it on the khata. Sukoon works out the total and the change, prints a receipt, and gives every sale a permanent invoice number.

Keeps your stock honest

Every sale takes items off the shelf count. Every delivery adds to it. Breakages and expiry are recorded separately, so at month end you can see what you lost rather than assuming you sold it.

Runs your khata

Every regular has a running balance. Purchases and payments are lines in their book. It tells you who is over their limit and who has not paid in a month.

Tells you how you are doing

Today's sales, what customers owe you, what your shelves are worth, what to reorder, and an estimate of your profit — all on one screen at closing time.

Two things it deliberately does not do

It does not need the internet to sell. The whole shop can trade with the internet down. The internet is used for one thing only: sending a locked copy of your books off the premises for safety (Section 13).

It does not let one person quietly change the books. Every sale, refund, payment and stock change is stamped with the name of the person who did it and the time they did it. Nothing is ever deleted — mistakes are corrected with a new entry that shows what happened. That is what makes the records worth keeping.

Why records that cannot be quietly changed matter

The most common way a shop loses money is not a burglary. It is small, repeated adjustments that nobody can trace afterwards. Sukoon's refusal to delete anything is not stubbornness — it is the whole point. If a number is wrong, the trail shows you who entered it and when.

2Before you begin

Get these five things ready and the installation takes about twenty minutes. Missing one of them is what turns it into an afternoon.

What you need

ThingWhat to check
The shop computer Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit. This is the machine that will hold your books, so choose the one that stays switched on and is not carried around. Click Start → Settings → System → About to check the version.
An administrator account on it You must be signed in to Windows as an administrator to install Sukoon. If you are not sure, it is usually the main account that was set up when the computer was bought.
The installer file Sukoon-Setup-1.1.0.exe, on a USB stick or downloaded. It is about 27 MB.
Your receipt printer Plugged into the shop computer by USB, and already working in Windows — meaning Windows can print a test page to it. Sukoon uses Windows' own printing, so if Windows cannot print, Sukoon cannot either.
Your Cloudflare backup details The four lines from the storage account you set up: access key ID, secret access key, bucket name and storage address. Section 13 explains where they go. If you do not have them to hand, install anyway and add them the same day.

Two settings on the computer, before you install

Both of these cause problems later that look like something else, so it is worth two minutes now.

  1. Check the date, time and time zone are right Start → Settings → Time & language → Date & time. Turn Set time automatically on, and make sure the time zone says Pakistan. Your receipts and your invoice numbering both follow the clock — a wrong time zone puts the wrong time on every receipt you print.
  2. Set the network to Private, not Public Start → Settings → Network & internet, click the network you are connected to, and choose Private network. On a Public network, Windows blocks your other counters from reaching Sukoon, and the symptom is a counter that simply says it cannot connect for no visible reason.

If you only have one counter

You can skip the network step and ignore Section 14 entirely. Sukoon works perfectly well on a single computer. You can always add a second counter later.

3Installing Sukoon

Windows will show you one frightening-looking warning during this. It is expected. Section 3 tells you exactly what it says and what to click.

  1. Double-click Sukoon-Setup-1.1.0.exe If Windows asks whether to allow the program to make changes, say yes.
  2. A blue box appears: "Windows protected your PC" Click the small More info link, then the Run anyway button that appears underneath. This is expected — see the note below.
  3. Accept every default, and tick the desktop shortcut There is nothing to choose. Do not change the folder it installs into.
  4. Wait for it to finish, and read any box that appears at the end If a box says "Sukoon is installed, but needs attention", it lists what to do — work through that list. If a box says "setting up its server did not finish", Sukoon will not run: stop and call your installer rather than trying again.

About the "Windows protected your PC" warning

This appears because the installer is not digitally signed — a signature is a certificate bought from a company Microsoft trusts, and it was a deliberate decision not to buy one for a single-shop program. The warning means Windows has not seen this file before. It does not mean anything is wrong with it.

Click More info, then Run anyway. If you never see this warning, you are probably running a different file than the one you were given — stop and check.

If your antivirus removes something

Some antivirus programs quarantine sukoon-server.exe or Sukoon.exe for the same reason — they are new and unsigned. If that happens, restore both files in your antivirus, add C:\Program Files\Sukoon to its exclusions, and run the installer again.

What the installer has done

Three things worth knowing, because they explain how Sukoon behaves afterwards:

  • Sukoon now starts by itself when the computer starts. Nobody has to sign in to Windows first. Switch the computer on in the morning and your counters can reach it about a minute later. This was tested: the server was answering 21 seconds after Windows had started, with nobody signed in.
  • Your books live in one fixed place: C:\ProgramData\Sukoon\. This cannot be moved, and it is locked so that only Windows administrators and Sukoon itself can open it.
  • Uninstalling Sukoon keeps your data. The folder above survives. But read the warning in Section 13 before you ever uninstall — some of your saved keys do not survive, and the recovery sheet becomes the only way back.

4The first time you open it

Five boxes to fill in, then one step that most people skip and later regret. Do not skip it.

  1. Open Sukoon from the desktop shortcut — on the shop computer itself The setup screen deliberately refuses to work from any other counter. If you try it from Till 2, it will tell you to go and finish setup on the shop PC. That is on purpose: the first owner account should not be creatable by whoever happens to be standing at another counter.
  2. The screen says Set up Sukoon. Fill in four boxes. Shop name — type it exactly as it should appear on every receipt and statement you hand a customer. Owner's name — your own name, as staff will see it on the sign-in screen. Password — at least 8 characters — and then Type it again.
  3. Press Set up Sukoon You land on the home screen. Down the side you should see Till, Stock, Khata, Refunds, Insights, Messages and Settings. If Khata or Insights are missing, something went wrong — stop and call your installer.

Choosing your password

This one password protects your entire business. Treat it like the key to the shop's safe, because that is what it is.

  • Do not use the shop's phone number, your own number, or the shop name.
  • Do not give it to staff so they can "just do one thing". Give them their own account instead — that takes one minute and is covered in Section 5.
  • Write it down somewhere at home, not at the counter.

Now create a second owner account. This is the most important thing in this section.

Sukoon has no password recovery. There is no reset link, no security question, no code to your phone. This is deliberate: anything that could let you back in could let someone else in too.

The consequence is simple. If you are the only Admin and you forget your password, or you are away and unreachable, nobody can get into Sukoon at all — not your staff, not your installer, not the person who wrote it.

So, right now: go to Settings → Manage staff → Add someone, and create a second Admin account for a person you trust completely — a partner, a son, a brother. Two Admins means one can always reset the other. Sukoon will not let you switch off or demote the last remaining Admin, precisely to stop you locking yourself out by accident.

While you are in Settings, set two more things

SettingWhat to do
Receipt printer Choose your printer under Which printer, save, then press Print a test receipt. If paper comes out, you are done. If it does not, see Section 9 — you can still trade.
Offsite backup Type in your four Cloudflare lines and press Send one now, then Bring one back and check it. Then print the recovery sheet. All of this is Section 13, and it matters more than anything else on this page.

5Your staff and their accounts

Everyone signs in as themselves. That single habit is what makes every other record in Sukoon trustworthy.

Staff do not type an email address or a username. The sign-in screen shows each person's name and initials; they tap their own and type a short password. It takes two seconds, which is the point — a sign-in that is slow is a sign-in that gets shared.

The two roles

RoleCan doCannot do
Cashier Ring up sales, take khata payments, open a new khata for a customer, look at stock, and request a refund. Change prices, approve refunds, see Insights or reports, add or change staff.
Admin Everything a cashier can, plus change prices, approve refunds, see Insights and reports, manage staff, and change settings. Nothing is hidden from an Admin.

Give each person the smaller role that still lets them do their job. Your counter staff should be Cashiers. Admin is for you and your second trusted person, and that is all.

Managing accounts

Everything is at Settings → Manage staff. Every change here asks for Your own password first — that is deliberate, so that someone who finds your screen unattended cannot quietly give themselves Admin.

What you wantWhat to do
Add a new person Add someone — their name, Cashier or Admin, and a password you choose. Tell them the password face to face, not on WhatsApp.
Someone forgot their password Set password on their row. This also clears a lockout.
Someone is locked out Five wrong passwords locks an account for 15 minutes. Either wait, or set a new password for them, which unlocks it immediately.
Promote a cashier Change role on their row.
Someone leaves the shop Switch their account off. Do not look for a delete button — there isn't one, on purpose. Their past sales and khata entries must stay in the books, so Sukoon keeps the person and simply stops them signing in.

Sukoon signs people out after about ten hours

Long enough for a full trading day, short enough that a counter left signed in overnight does not stay open. Staff will need to sign in again the next morning — this is normal.

6Getting your shop into the computer

This is the one genuinely large job, and it happens once. Everything after it is quick.

Nothing can be sold until it exists in the catalogue. The catalogue is the list of everything your shop sells — separately from whether any of it is on the shelf right now.

The fast way in: Bulk entry

Stock → Bulk entry is built for exactly this day. Scan a barcode, type a name, type a selling price, press Enter — and the cursor jumps straight back to the barcode box, ready for the next item. You can do several hundred items without ever touching the mouse.

Only two things are required: the name and the selling price. Everything else — cost price, category, the low-stock warning level — can be filled in later. Sukoon puts an amber Needs completing badge on anything that is missing them. That badge is a to-do list, not an error. The item sells perfectly well.

How to approach the first day

Do not try to enter 2,000 lines perfectly before you start trading. Enter your fastest-moving few hundred items with just name and price, and start selling. When a cashier scans something that is not in the catalogue yet, Sukoon lets them create it on the spot mid-sale — and marks it Needs completing for you to tidy up later. Within a fortnight your catalogue fills itself.

Careful, one at a time

Stock → New product is the full form, for when you want to set everything properly:

FieldWhat it means
Name and priceRequired. The price is what you charge.
Cost priceWhat you paid your supplier. Optional — but until you enter it, Sukoon cannot tell you what your shelves are worth or estimate your profit. It will show nothing rather than guess.
Sealed pack or looseA bottle of oil is a sealed pack — you sell 1, 2, 3, never 1.4. Atta scooped from a sack is loose — 1.4 kg is normal. Sukoon refuses fractions on a sealed pack and insists on a weight for a loose item.
UnitWhat one of them is: bottle, kg, packet, unit. Purely so the screen reads properly.
CategoryOptional. Groups items on reports and generates the internal code.
Low-stock level"Warn me when this drops to 6." Optional, and worth setting only on items you must never run out of.

Barcodes and labels

One product can have several barcodes. A 1-litre bottle and its six-pack carton scan differently but are the same product, so you add both barcodes to the one item.

Sukoon also gives every product its own internal code — something like GRO-0001. For loose goods and anything that came without a printed barcode, open Stock → Labels and Sukoon produces an A4 sheet of barcodes you print onto sticker paper with an ordinary printer. No special label printer is needed.

7Stock: deliveries, losses, reordering

Three words cover everything here: stock in, stock out, and the movement history that ties them together.

When a delivery arrives

Open the product, press Stock in, type how many arrived, and give a reason. Twenty-four bottles of oil arrived: stock in, 24, "supplier delivery". That is the whole job.

When goods leave without being sold

Breakage, expiry, theft, or you taking a bottle home — that is a Stock out, with a reason. Always record these. A sale also lowers the shelf count, but Sukoon records it as its own separate kind of movement, and that difference is the entire point: at month end you can see that you lost Rs 3,000 to expiry, instead of quietly believing you sold it.

The movement history — your audit trail

Every product page has a Recent stock movements table. It is that item's whole life story: every delivery, every loss, every sale, who did it, when, and why. When a number looks wrong, this table is where the answer is. Nothing is ever removed from it.

The four tiles at the top of the Stock screen

TileWhat it counts
Catalog sizeHow many different products you sell.
Needs attentionItems that are running low — below the warning level you set, but not yet finished.
Out of stockItems that are finished. Reorder these now.
Stock valueWhat everything on your shelves cost you — at cost price, not what you sell it for. This answers "how much of my money is sitting in this room?"

If Stock value says Rs 0 and your shelves are full

That is correct behaviour, not a fault. Stock value is measured at cost price, and you have not entered cost prices yet — that is what the amber Needs completing badges are tracking. Sukoon will not value your shelf at the retail price and flatter you with a number that is not true. Enter your cost prices and the figure appears.

The reordering habit

At the end of each day, glance at the Stock screen. The items with coral bars are the ones running low or finished. That list is your supplier order. That is the whole loop — everything else in Sukoon hangs off it.

8Selling: the Till

The screen your staff will spend the day on. Learn it once yourself before you teach it to anyone.

The very first time: name the counter

Each counter asks Name this till once, and you type Till 1, Till 2 and so on. It never asks again. That name prints on every receipt from that counter — so when Rs 400 is missing at the end of a day, you can tell which counter rang the sale.

Ringing up a customer

  1. Scan or type each item A sealed pack appears with a − 3 + stepper for the quantity. A loose item opens a small weight box instead, because the assistant has to walk to the scale and come back. You can type the weight in kg, or press by amount and type the rupees the customer asked for — Sukoon works out the weight.
  2. If it says "Waiting on 1 weight", go and weigh it The Complete sale button stays dead until every weight is filled in. This is deliberate — it stops a sale going through with an item that was never actually weighed.
  3. Choose how they are paying Cash, Card, or Khata. A walk-in customer paying cash needs nothing else — no name, no phone number. That is the default because it is the overwhelming majority of a general store's sales.
  4. For cash, tap the note they handed you Beside Cash received are shortcut buttons: Exact, then the next round Rs 100, Rs 500, Rs 1,000. On a Rs 255 bill they read Exact · Rs 300 · Rs 500. Tap the one matching the note they gave you and Change to return shows what to hand back — no mental arithmetic with a queue watching.
  5. Press Complete sale The sale is now permanent. The receipt prints, the shelf counts come down, and the Sale complete screen shows the change to return. It moves on to the next sale by itself after a few seconds, or press Start next sale.

Things the till will stop you doing, and why

What you seeWhy
"Only 4 in stock" You cannot sell more of a counted item than you have. If the shelf really does have more, your count is wrong — do a stock in to correct it, with a reason.
An Admin password is demanded mid-sale This khata customer would go past their credit limit. See Section 10.
Complete sale stays grey Something is unfinished — usually a weight not yet entered. The amber line above the total says what.

A cart is not a sale

Items scanned but not yet paid for are just a scratchpad. No invoice exists, nothing has left your stock. Closing the browser mid-sale loses the cart — which costs nothing, because nothing had happened yet. Once you press Complete sale, it is permanent.

Invoice numbers

Every completed sale gets a number like INV-2026-0001. They are never reused and never skipped, even if two counters complete a sale at the same instant. That gap-free run of numbers is what makes your books auditable — and it is what a customer quotes when they come back for a refund.

9Receipts and the printer

Set the printer up once. If it ever fails, your sales carry on regardless.

Setting it up

Go to Settings → Receipt printer, pick your printer under Which printer, save, and press Print a test receipt. If paper comes out, you are finished.

Sukoon prints through Windows' own printing system, using your printer's own driver. This means two things: your printer must be installed and working in Windows first, and it should be installed for the whole computer, not just for your Windows user — if it was added under one Windows account only, Sukoon may not be able to see it.

Honest note: printing has never been tested on your actual printer

Everything in the printing path was built and tested, but never against the physical printer in your shop, because its make and model were not known while Sukoon was being written. Your first test receipt is genuinely the first test.

If it does not work, nothing is lost — see below. Tell your installer the printer's make and model and it will be sorted out.

If the printer does not work

You will see Printer unavailable after a sale. Note carefully what this does not mean:

  • The sale is complete and saved. Stock has come down, the khata is updated, the invoice number is issued. Only the paper failed.
  • You can still give the customer a receipt. Sukoon offers it as a PDF, which you can open and print on any ordinary printer, or show on screen.
  • A sale is never held up by a printer. Keep serving customers and deal with the printer afterwards.

You can also reprint any receipt later with Print again on the Sale complete screen.

10Khata: your credit book

The same book you have always kept, with three things added: it adds up by itself, it warns you before someone goes too far, and it prints a statement the customer cannot argue with.

Opening a khata

In Khata → Add customer: name, and their phone number. Sukoon will warn you if another customer already has that number — but it will still let you save it, because in a real neighbourhood a father and son genuinely do share a phone.

One customer, several khatas. A customer can keep separate khatas on his own number — one for home, one for his office, one for a brother he pays for. Open each one with the same name and phone, and give it a Label: Home, Office, Brother – Bilal. Sukoon then shows it everywhere as Ali Raza (Home), so the cashier picks the right one at the till. Each khata keeps its own balance and its own credit limit. If the number is already confirmed on his first khata, the new one is confirmed too, and every WhatsApp message says which khata it is about.

Confirming the number matters. When you know the number is genuinely theirs — because they showed you the phone, or you rang it in front of them — mark it confirmed with They showed it or I rang it. Until a number is confirmed, Sukoon will never send balances or amounts to it. An unconfirmed number is treated as possibly belonging to a stranger, and a stranger must not learn what your customer owes.

Credit limits

Every new khata starts at Rs 10,000. You can change it per customer.

When a sale would push a customer past their limit, the till stops. A cashier cannot go past it on their own. An Admin standing at the counter can approve it by typing their own name and password — and that approval is written permanently into the customer's book, with the Admin's name against it.

Why a limit that can be overridden is still worth having

The limit does not exist to refuse your customers. It exists so that extending more credit is a decision someone made, on the record, rather than something that happened quietly over four months because nobody was adding up. That is the difference between a bad debt you chose and a bad debt you discovered.

Taking a payment

Open the customer and press Record payment. Enter the amount, choose how they paid — cash, bank transfer, JazzCash or Easypaisa, or card — and add a reference if there is one. The balance updates immediately.

  • Any amount is fine. They owe Rs 4,000 and hand you Rs 1,500 — record Rs 1,500.
  • If they pay more than they owe, Sukoon asks you to confirm, then keeps the extra as In credit, shown on their page and used up automatically by their next purchase.

Overdue customers

You can set credit terms per customer — "settle within 30 days". If they owe money and have not made any payment in that many days, their khata is flagged overdue on the Khata screen. Any payment, even a small one, restarts the count. This is the list you work through when you have a quiet hour.

Statements

Open a customer, choose a month, and press Statement. You get a printable page showing what they owed at the start, every purchase and payment in between, and what they owe at the end. Download it as a PDF to print or send. This is the document that ends arguments.

The Khata screen at a glance

Currently owed is the money out on the street right now. Near credit limit and Settled let you filter the list. Recent activity shows the last purchases and payments across all customers — a quick way to see that the day's credit sales were recorded properly.

11Refunds

A refund always needs two people. This is the single most deliberate design decision in Sukoon, and it is there to protect you.

A cashier records the refund. An Admin approves it. Until it is approved, nothing moves — no cash out, no stock back, no change to anyone's khata.

How a refund is recorded

  1. The cashier opens Refunds and types the invoice number From the customer's receipt. The original sale must be found — Sukoon will not refund money against nothing.
  2. Pick the lines and the quantities coming back You do not have to undo the whole sale. If they bought three bottles and brought one back, refund one.
  3. Decide per item whether it can be sold again There is a restock tickbox on each line. Ticked, the item goes back on the shelf count. Unticked — because it came back broken or expired — the customer gets their money and your shelf count correctly does not go up.
  4. Give a reason, and save The cashier's job ends here. The refund now sits as pending approval.
  5. An Admin opens Refunds → pending and approves it With their own password. Only now does the money, the stock and the khata actually move.

Why this is worth the extra thirty seconds

A false refund is the most common way staff take money from a shop: ring a refund for goods nobody returned, and take the cash out of the drawer. If one person can do that alone, it is invisible. Requiring a second person's password makes it a conspiracy rather than an opportunity — and the approval is recorded with that person's name against it.

Do not be tempted to give your cashiers Admin rights so they can approve their own refunds. That single shortcut removes the protection entirely.

"Refundable 0.1" when they bought 1.4 kg

Refundable means what is still available to refund on that line: sold, minus what has already been refunded, minus anything already requested but not yet approved. If it reads lower than you expect, there are pending requests waiting for an Admin. Approve or reject those first.

12Insights and reports

Admin only. Five minutes at closing time, and you will know your shop better than a month of guessing.

Insights — the closing-time screen

Insights shows the day on one page: what you sold, where the sales came from (cash, card, khata), what customers owe you, and an estimate of your profit. You can switch it between Today, This week, This month or your own dates, and it compares against the same point in the previous period so you can see whether you are up or down.

The five reports

ReportAnswers
SalesWhat sold, when, and how it was paid for.
Estimated profitRoughly what you made — see the note below.
Stock valueWhat is on your shelves, at what it cost you.
Low stockYour reorder list.
Outstanding creditEvery customer who owes you money, and how much.

Every report can be downloaded as a PDF to print, or as a CSV to open in Excel. What you see on screen, what prints, and what downloads are always the same figures.

Why it says "estimated" profit

Profit is worked out using today's cost prices, not the cost price on the day each item actually sold — Sukoon does not track what you paid for each individual delivery. If your supplier's prices have moved, the figure drifts.

Also, any item with no cost price entered is left out of the profit figure entirely (though its sale still counts in your sales total). The report tells you what proportion of your sales it could actually cost, so you always know how much to trust it. The more cost prices you fill in, the truer the number becomes.

This is deliberate. A profit figure that quietly guessed at the missing costs would look more confident and be worth less.

The closing routine that costs one minute

Open Insights. Look at three numbers: today's sales, today's credit given, and today's cash. If any of them is not roughly what your day felt like, something is worth checking tonight rather than at the end of the month.

13Backups — read this one twice

Everything else in this manual is about running your shop well. This section is about not losing it.

A computer can be stolen, dropped, flooded or simply die one morning with no warning. If that happens and your only copy of the books was inside it, your khata is gone and there is no way to reconstruct it. Sukoon's answer is two layers, and one printed page.

Layer one: on the shop computer, every 15 minutes

Throughout trading hours, Sukoon saves a complete copy of your books into a folder on the shop PC, and again at closing. This protects you against the ordinary things — a mistake, a power cut mid-sale, a file going wrong. It does not protect you against the computer itself being lost, because the copies are inside it.

Layer two: off the premises, over the internet

Each of those copies is then locked with a key that only your shop holds, and sent to your own Cloudflare storage account on the internet. If the shop PC is stolen or burns, your books are still safe somewhere else — and locked, so the storage company cannot read them either.

Set this up in Settings → Offsite backup, where you type in four things from your Cloudflare account: Access key ID, Secret access key, Bucket name and Storage address. Then press Send one now, and then Bring one back and check it — which fetches a copy back, unlocks it and confirms it can actually be read. Sukoon repeats that check by itself once a week.

The recovery sheet — the single most important page you own

Print it today. Take it home today.

Go to Settings → Recovery sheet and press Print. That one page carries the key that unlocks your off-site backups.

Without it, those backups can never be opened — by anyone. Not by you, not by your installer, not by the person who built Sukoon, not by Cloudflare. There is no master key and no back door. That is exactly why the backups are safe to keep on the internet at all.

What to do: keep it at home or with your property papers, away from the shop. Keep a second copy in a different safe place if you can.

What never to do: do not photograph it. Do not send it on WhatsApp or email. Do not keep it in the shop, where the same fire or the same thief would take both it and the computer.

Check it now and then

Settings → Backups shows when the last backup worked. Glance at it once a week. If it turns red, tell your installer that day — a backup that quietly stopped working three months ago is the same as having no backup, and you only find out on the day you need it.

Two warnings worth knowing in advance

If Sukoon says this computer cannot read the backup key

This can happen after certain repairs to Windows or a change to the computer. Sukoon will tell you plainly rather than crash, and it will never quietly make a new key — because a new key would leave all your older backups locked forever without telling you.

The fix is the recovery sheet: type the key from it back in at Settings → Offsite backup → Use this key. There is also a Start a new key button — do not press it unless your installer tells you to. It warns you first, because your older off-site backups stay locked to the old key.

Never uninstall Sukoon to "fix" something

Uninstalling keeps your books, but it removes the Windows account Sukoon uses — and the saved keys (your storage secret, the backup key) are tied to that account and become unreadable. Your data survives, but the route back to your off-site backups would be the printed recovery sheet and nothing else. If something is wrong, call your installer.

14Adding the other counters

Skip this section entirely if you only have one counter.

The other counters do not have Sukoon installed on them. They simply open a web browser — Edge or Chrome — and look at the shop computer. That is why the shop computer must stay switched on, and why everything you read on Till 2 is instantly the same as on Till 1.

  1. Find the shop computer's address It is shown on the Settings screen, under The other tills. It looks like http://192.168.1.50:5000.
  2. Type that address into the browser on each other counter, and bookmark it Make it the browser's home page so it opens there every morning.
  3. Name each counter when it asks Till 2, Till 3. Once only.
  4. Ring one real sale from every counter before you rely on it Then refund it. It is worth five minutes to find a problem now rather than with customers waiting.

One job for your installer: fixing the address

That address is handed out by your router, and routers sometimes hand out a different one after a power cut — at which point the other counters point at nothing and say they cannot connect. The fix is to tell the router to always give the shop PC the same address. It takes five minutes but needs the router's password, so ask your installer to do it. Until it is done, a power cut may mean re-typing the address on the other counters.

Worth proving once: the morning restart

Restart the shop computer and do not sign in to Windows. Within about a minute, the other counters should reach Sukoon again by themselves. If they do, your mornings will always work, whoever opens up.

15What is not switched on yet

An honest list. Everything here is built and tested, but held back until something outside the software is ready.

FeatureStatusWhat is waiting
WhatsApp messages to customers Off Sukoon can message khata customers when they buy on credit, when they pay, when a statement is due, and when they fall overdue. It is written and tested. It stays switched off until your WhatsApp Business account exists and one real message has been sent and seen arriving on a real phone. Nothing is sent to anyone until you have watched it work.
Printing on your printer Untested Built and tested, but never against your actual printer — its model was not known while Sukoon was written. Your first test receipt is the real test. PDF receipts work regardless, and a sale is never blocked by a printer.
Discounts Not built Deliberately not built, and confirmed twice. The till shows both Subtotal and Total due and today they are always identical — the line is there so the screen does not jump if discounts are ever added.
Tax calculation By design Your prices already include tax, so Sukoon calculates nothing. Receipts say Tax included.
Weighing scale connected to the PC Not built Weights are typed in by hand. The groundwork for connecting a scale later is in place, but no scale hardware was bought.
Automatic updates Not built Sukoon will never update itself behind your back. New versions are installed by hand, deliberately, with a backup taken first.

One thing you should know about going live

Sukoon is going into your shop without a supervised trial period alongside your paper books. That was your decision and it was recorded as such — but it means your shop is the first real test of the whole system working together.

What to do about it: for the first two weeks, keep a rough paper note of each day's takings and check it against Insights at closing. If anything ever disagrees — a balance that looks wrong, a figure that does not match — write down the invoice number and what you expected, and call your installer. Do not delete anything. The trail is what makes the problem findable.

16Your daily routine

Five minutes a day is all Sukoon asks of you.

Opening

Leave the shop computer switched on, or switch it on and wait a minute. Nobody needs to sign in to Windows. Each person signs in to Sukoon as themselves at their own counter.

During the day

Ring every sale through the till, including the small ones. Record deliveries as they arrive rather than at the end of the week. Record breakages and expiry the moment they happen.

Closing

Open Insights. Check today's sales, today's credit and today's cash against what the day felt like. Glance at the Stock screen for your reorder list.

Once a week

Check Settings → Backups is green. Work through the overdue customers on the Khata screen. Fill in cost prices for a few more products.

The rules worth insisting on with staff

  • Nobody uses anyone else's account. Ever. The moment two people share a login, every record in Sukoon becomes an opinion.
  • Every sale goes through the till, including "just one packet for the boy outside". An item that leaves without being rung up is a stock count that drifts, and a difference you can never explain later.
  • Breakage and expiry get recorded as stock out, with a reason. Nobody is in trouble for a broken bottle. They should be in trouble for hiding it, because a hidden loss looks exactly like theft in the figures.
  • Cashiers request refunds; you approve them. No exceptions, and no giving a cashier Admin rights to save a walk to the counter.

A monthly habit worth starting

On the first of the month, print statements for your khata customers who owe the most and hand them over. It takes fifteen minutes, it is far harder to argue with than a handwritten page, and shops that do this get paid noticeably faster.

17When something goes wrong

Most problems are one of these six. The last column is the one that matters.

What you seeWhat to do
A counter says it cannot connect Is the shop computer switched on? Restart it and wait a minute. If only one counter is affected, check that counter's network cable or wifi. If all of them are, check the shop PC's network is still set to Private (Section 2).
"Printer unavailable" after a sale The sale is complete and saved. Use the PDF receipt button. Carry on serving customers and tell your installer afterwards.
Sukoon says its server is not running Restart the computer. If it says it again after restarting, call your installer. Do not uninstall or reinstall anything yourself — see Section 13.
A balance or a figure looks wrong Write down the invoice number, the customer, and what you expected. Then call your installer. Do not delete or adjust anything first — the trail is what makes it findable, and adjusting it destroys the evidence.
Someone is locked out Five wrong passwords locks an account for 15 minutes. Wait, or set them a new password at Settings → Manage staff.
The backups panel has turned red Tell your installer the same day. Everything still works, but you are running without a safety net until it is fixed.

Four things never to do

1. Never uninstall or reinstall Sukoon to try to fix a problem.
2. Never delete a customer, a product or a sale to make a number look right. Correct it with a new entry instead.
3. Never share an Admin password with counter staff.
4. Never keep the recovery sheet in the shop, and never photograph it.

What to have ready when you call

What you were doing, what the screen said (word for word if you can, or a photo of it), and the invoice number if a sale was involved. That is usually enough to find the answer without anyone visiting the shop.

18The words on the screen

Every term Sukoon uses, in plain English. Most of them are the words of the trade already — that is deliberate.

At the counter

TillA checkout counter — the counter plus the computer on it. Each one names itself once so you can tell which counter rang a sale.
CartItems scanned but not yet paid for. Nothing has left your stock and no invoice exists — it is a scratchpad until you press Complete sale.
Walk-in customerSomeone who pays now and leaves. No account, no name needed. The default, because it is most of your sales.
Cash receivedThe note the customer actually handed you. Rs 2,000 for a Rs 1,700 bill.
Change to returnWhat you hand back. Rs 300 in that example.
ExactA shortcut meaning they gave you the precise amount. The buttons beside it are the next round notes.
Invoice numberLike INV-2026-0001. Permanent, never reused, never skipped. What a customer quotes for a refund.
Subtotal / Total dueThe goods added up, and what they owe. Identical today, because there are no discounts.

The goods

CatalogueEverything your shop sells — whether or not any is on the shelf today. Oil stays in the catalogue at zero stock; you have not stopped selling it, you have run out.
Sealed pack / looseA bottle you sell whole, versus atta you scoop by weight. Sukoon refuses fractions on the first and insists on a weight for the second.
Stock in / Stock outGoods arriving, and goods leaving for any reason other than a sale — breakage, expiry, theft, your own use.
Stock levelHow many are on the shelf right now. Never goes below zero.
Low-stock level"Warn me when this drops to 6." Optional, per product.
Stock valueWhat your shelves cost you, at cost price — not at what you sell for.
SKU / barcodeThe SKU (GRO-0001) is Sukoon's own code for the item. The barcode is the manufacturer's printed one. A product can have several barcodes.
Needs completingAn amber badge: this item was created in a hurry and is missing its cost price, category or warning level. It sells fine. It is a to-do list, not a fault.

Credit and refunds

KhataThe credit book. Each regular has a running balance; every purchase and payment is a line in it.
Credit limitThe most a customer may owe. Rs 10,000 by default. Going past it needs an Admin's password at the counter.
Credit terms / overdue"Settle within 30 days." If they owe and have not paid in that long, they are flagged overdue. Any payment restarts the count.
In creditThey paid more than they owed, so you owe them. Used up by their next purchase.
StatementA printable page for one customer and one month: opening balance, every line, closing balance.
Confirmed numberA phone number you have verified is really theirs. Unconfirmed numbers are never sent balances or amounts.
RestockThe tickbox on a refund. Ticked: it goes back on the shelf. Unticked: it came back broken, so the customer is paid but your shelf count correctly does not rise.
Pending approvalA refund a cashier has recorded and an Admin has not yet approved. Nothing has moved yet.
RefundableHow much of a line can still be refunded: sold, less already refunded, less already requested.

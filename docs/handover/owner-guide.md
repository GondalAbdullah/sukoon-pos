# Sukoon — what the shop owner needs to know

Print this and keep it near the counter.

## Every day

**Leave the shop computer switched on.** Sukoon starts by itself when the computer starts — nobody
has to sign in first. The other counters just open Sukoon in their browser.

**Everyone signs in as themselves.** Every sale, payment and refund is recorded with the name of
the person who did it. That is what makes the records worth having, so nobody should use someone
else's account.

**At closing, open Insights.** Today's sales, what customers owe you, and today's estimated profit.
It takes a minute and it is the quickest way to notice something wrong.

## Adding and removing staff

**Settings → Manage staff.**

- **Add someone:** their name, whether they are a cashier or an admin, and a password you choose.
  Tell them the password in person.
- **Someone forgot their password, or is locked out** after too many wrong tries: set a new one on
  the same screen.
- **Someone leaves:** switch their account off. Do not try to delete them — their past sales and
  Khata entries must stay, and Sukoon keeps them.

A **cashier** can sell, take payments, open Khatas and ask for refunds. An **admin** can also change
prices, approve refunds, see Insights and add staff. Give people the smallest role that lets them do
their job.

## Money and Khata

- **Refunds need two people.** A cashier records the refund; an admin approves it with their
  password. Nothing moves — no cash, no stock, no Khata — until it is approved.
- **New Khata customers start with a Rs 10,000 limit.** A sale that would take them over it stops
  and asks for an admin's password. That is deliberate: the limit exists so that going past it is a
  decision, not an accident.
- **A customer can pay any amount.** If they pay more than they owe, Sukoon keeps the extra as
  credit for their next purchase.
- **Statements:** open a customer, choose a month, and download the PDF to print or hand over.

## Backups — the part that matters most

Sukoon saves a copy of your books **every fifteen minutes**, and sends a locked copy **off the
premises** over the internet. If the computer is stolen or the disk dies, your books survive.

**Two things you must do:**

1. **Keep the recovery sheet away from the shop.** It is the one page with the key that unlocks your
   offsite backups. Without it, nobody can read them — not even the person who built Sukoon. Keep it
   at home or with your important papers. Do not photograph it or send it on WhatsApp.
2. **Look at Settings → Backups now and then.** It says when the last backup worked. If it turns red,
   tell whoever looks after Sukoon.

## What is not switched on yet

- **WhatsApp messages to customers.** Everything is built, but it cannot be switched on until your
  WhatsApp Business account exists and one real message has been sent and checked.
- **Printed receipts** may need setting up with your printer. Until then, Sukoon offers a receipt
  you can open and print as a PDF. Sales are never held up by the printer.

## When something goes wrong

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| A counter says it can't connect | Is the shop computer on? Restart it and wait a minute. If only one counter is affected, check its cable |
| "Printer unavailable" after a sale | The sale is complete and saved. Use the PDF receipt button; tell your installer |
| Sukoon says it can't start | Restart the computer. If it says it again, call your installer and don't reinstall anything yourself |
| A number looks wrong | Write down the invoice number and what you expected, and tell your installer. Do not delete anything |

**Sukoon never loses a completed sale.** If a printer, a message or the internet fails, the sale is
already saved. The rest can be fixed afterwards.

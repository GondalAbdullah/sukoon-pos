Part 1

The shop and the problem

Before any feature makes sense, you need a picture of the place it runs in. Sukoon was not designed for "a store" in the abstract. It was designed for one particular kind of shop.

What Al-Rehman General Store is

A general store, called a kiryana store in Pakistan, is the neighbourhood shop that sells everyday things: flour (atta), rice, lentils (daal), cooking oil and ghee, milk and yogurt, soap, biscuits, cold drinks. It is usually run by the owner with one or two helpers behind a counter, and customers ask for things rather than walking aisles.

Four facts about this kind of shop shaped almost every design decision in Sukoon:

  • Two kinds of goods. Some things come in sealed packs you count (a bottle of oil). Others are scooped from a sack and weighed (1.5 kg of atta). The software has to handle both at the same counter.
  • Two kinds of customers. Most people pay on the spot and leave (walk-in customers). But trusted regulars take goods now and pay later, and the shop keeps their account in a Khata, a credit book. Losing track of who owes what is one of the biggest real risks to a small shop's money.
  • Unreliable internet and power. The shop cannot stop selling because the internet is down. So everything except WhatsApp messages works completely offline, on one PC inside the shop.
  • Small staff, high trust, real temptation. A cashier handles cash all day. The system has to make honest mistakes easy to catch and dishonest ones hard to hide, without insulting good staff. That's why some actions (refunds, price changes) need the owner's approval.

What the shop needs software for

Problem without softwareWhat Sukoon does about it
Adding up bills by hand; wrong change under pressureThe till totals every sale and works out the change
Not knowing what's on the shelf until it runs outEvery item's stock goes down as it sells; low and out-of-stock items are flagged
Goods going missing with no recordEvery stock change has a reason and a person's name on it
The credit book gets lost, smudged or argued aboutEach Khata has a ledger of every purchase and payment, with printable statements
Customers forget what they oweWhatsApp messages after purchases and payments, and monthly statements
The owner doesn't know if the shop made money todayThe Insights dashboard: today's sales, estimated profit, credit outstanding

What Sukoon is, in one paragraph

Sukoon is a point-of-sale (POS) system: the software behind the counter where sales happen. Around it sit inventory (stock tracking), credit customer management (Khata), customer messaging (WhatsApp) and reports. It runs on one Windows PC in the shop. Other counters (tills) in the shop simply open it in a web browser over the shop's local network. No data leaves the shop except the WhatsApp messages.

"Sukoon" is an Urdu word meaning peace of mind, which is exactly what the owner should feel about the numbers.

Part 2

The words of the trade

Every word you'll see on Sukoon's screens and in its documents, grouped by where you meet them. These aren't software jargon. Most are how shopkeepers have talked for generations, which is exactly why the app uses them.

At the counter
Point of sale (POS)
The place where a sale is completed, and by extension the software used there. In Sukoon, the Till screen is the POS.
Till · terminal · counter
Historically the cash drawer; now the counter plus the computer or browser at it. A shop can have several. Each till names itself once ("Till 1", "Till 2") so every sale records which counter rang it.When Rs 400 is missing at night, the till name tells you which counter to look at.
Ring up
To enter a customer's items and take their payment: the whole job of the till.
Cart
The items scanned for the current customer, before they've paid. Nothing is saved to the books until the sale is completed; until then it's a scratchpad that lives in the browser session.
Walk-in customer
Someone who pays now and leaves: no name, no account. The default for every sale, because it's most of them.
Cash received · tendered
The money the customer physically hands over, e.g. a Rs 2,000 note for a Rs 1,700 bill.
Change
Cash received minus the total: Rs 300 in that example. Sukoon works it out.
Exact · quick amounts
The shortcut buttons under "Cash received": Exact (no change needed), then the next round Rs 100 / 500 / 1,000 / 5,000, meaning the notes people really carry. Duplicates are removed.A Rs 3,470 bill offers Exact · Rs 3,500 · Rs 4,000.
Invoice number
The permanent number of a completed sale, like INV-2026-0001. Never skipped and never reused, even with two tills selling at the same instant. It restarts at 0001 each year. A gap-free sequence is what makes the books auditable.
Receipt
The printed slip the customer takes: shop name, items, total, payment, change, invoice number, till name and time.
Subtotal / total
Subtotal is the goods added up; total is what's owed after discounts. In Sukoon they are always equal, because discounts were deliberately left out of version one.
Goods and stock
Catalogue
The list of everything the shop sells, whether or not any is on the shelf right now. An out-of-stock item stays in the catalogue.
Product
One entry in the catalogue: a name, a selling price, and optionally a cost price, category, barcodes and low-stock threshold.
Sealed pack goods
Items sold as whole countable units: a bottle, a tin, a packet. Quantities are whole numbers (1, 2, 3).
Loose goods · weighed goods
Items scooped and weighed at the counter: atta, rice, daal, sugar. Quantities can be fractions (1.5 kg).
Unit label
What one of a product is, for reading: bottle, kg, packet, unit.
Selling price / cost price
Selling price is what the customer pays. Cost price is what the shop paid the supplier. The gap between them is the shop's margin. The cashier often doesn't know the cost price, so it's optional.
Category
A group for organising the catalogue: "Groceries & Grains", "Cooking Oils & Ghee". Used in reports and to build SKUs.
Stock level
How many of a product are physically in the shop right now. Sukoon never lets it go below zero.
Stock in
Goods arriving, usually a supplier's delivery. Raises the stock level.
Stock out
Goods leaving for any reason other than a sale: breakage, expiry, theft, the owner taking some home. Lowers the level. Kept separate from sales so a loss never looks like a sale.
Correct count
Setting the stock level to what's actually counted on the shelf, when the numbers have drifted. Sukoon works out and records the difference.
Stock movement · audit trail
A permanent record of one change to a product's stock: when, what type, how much, the level after, the reason, and who did it. Together they form the product's complete history.
Low-stock threshold
"Warn me when this drops to 6." Set per product, and optional: no threshold means no warning.
Out of stock · restock
A counted product at zero. Shown in coral: reorder now.
Counted / never counted
A product is counted once someone has recorded a delivery or a count for it. Until then it's never counted, and its "0" means "unknown", not "none". The till only limits quantities for counted products.
Found at the till
When a never-counted product is sold, Sukoon automatically records the sold quantity as a stock-in labelled "Found at the till" first, so the books stay honest and never go negative.
Stock value
What the shelf is worth at cost price: the shop's money tied up in goods. A product with no cost price shows "value unknown", never zero.
Provisional product · needs completing
A product missing its cost price, category or threshold, typically created in a hurry at the till or through bulk entry. It sells normally; it's a to-do for the owner, shown with an amber badge.
Codes on goods and shelves
Barcode
The striped code printed on a product by its manufacturer. A scanner reads it and "types" it into the till.
SKU · stock keeping unit
The shop's own internal code for a product, like COO-0012. Sukoon makes it from the first three letters of the category plus a shop-wide running number (GEN when there's no category). Useful for shelves and stock-taking.
Internal barcode
A barcode Sukoon generates for goods that have none (loose goods in bags, local products), starting SK-, printed on sticker labels.
Price-override barcode
A second barcode on the same product that sells it at a different price, without splitting its stock.A "Near expiry, Oct" sticker that sells the same milk cheaper.
Keyboard wedge
How an ordinary USB barcode scanner works: the computer sees it as a keyboard that types the code very fast and presses Enter.
Money
Rupee and paisa
1 Pakistani rupee = 100 paisa. Sukoon stores every amount as a whole number of paisa (Rs 170 is stored as 17000), but every price and total a person sees is whole rupees.
Round at the line
When a weighed quantity makes a price like Rs 159.50, it's rounded to whole rupees once, on that line (half goes up: Rs 160), never again on the total. That's what a person at a counter expects.
By weight / by amount
Two ways to sell loose goods. By weight: type 1.5 kg and Sukoon works out the price. By amount: the customer says "give me Rs 200 of atta", you type Rs 200, and Sukoon works out the weight. In by-amount, the Rs 200 is exactly what's charged.
Payment method
How a sale is paid: cash, card, or Khata (on credit).
Margin
Profit as a percentage of sales. Rs 160 profit on Rs 1,200 of sales is a 13.3% margin.
Credit (Khata)
Khata · کھاتا · credit account
The account a shop keeps for a trusted regular who "puts it on the book": takes goods now, settles later. Kept as the Urdu word throughout the app on purpose.
Credit sale
A sale where no money changes hands; the total is added to the customer's Khata balance.
Balance
What the customer owes right now: purchases minus payments minus refunds.
Ledger
The list of every change to a Khata balance (purchase, payment, refund), each with the balance after it. The ledger is the truth; the balance is just its running total.
Credit limit
The most a customer may owe. New Khatas start at Rs 10,000. A sale past the limit is stopped unless the owner approves it at the counter. "Near credit limit" means 80% or more is used.
Credit terms
The agreed time to pay, in days, e.g. 30. Optional.
Overdue
A customer who owes money and hasn't paid anything within their credit terms, counted from their last payment (or their first credit purchase if they've never paid). Any payment, however small, restarts the count.
In credit
The customer paid more than they owed, so the shop owes them. Always shown as "Rs 200 in credit", never as a minus. Used up by their next purchase.
Statement
A printable summary of one customer's Khata for a month or all time: opening balance, every purchase and payment, closing balance.
Confirmed number
A phone number the cashier checked in person: the customer showed it on their phone, or the cashier rang it. Money messages only ever go to confirmed numbers.
Archived Khata
A customer removed from the lists with their history kept. Only possible at a zero balance.
Returns
Refund
Money (or Khata credit) given back for part or all of an earlier sale, found by its invoice number.
Pending approval
A refund a cashier has recorded but the owner hasn't approved. Nothing moves (no cash, no stock, no ledger change) until it's approved.
Restock
A tick on each refunded item: ticked means it came back sellable and goes back on the shelf; unticked means it was damaged, so the customer is refunded but stock doesn't go up.
Refundable
How much of a sale line can still be refunded: sold, minus already refunded, minus refunds still waiting for approval.
Step-up
Re-entering your password for one sensitive action (approving a refund, changing a price) even though you're already signed in. It proves the person at the keyboard is really the owner.
Messages and reports
WhatsApp consent · the tick
"Send updates on WhatsApp", ticked only when the customer agrees. Without it, Sukoon sends that customer nothing.
Message template
Fixed wording, with blanks for amounts and names, that WhatsApp must approve before a business may send it.
Account notice
The one neutral message a ticked customer with an unconfirmed number can get: "an account was opened in your name… if this is not you, contact us". No amounts, ever.
Reply-to number
The shop's everyday phone number, printed in every message, because the sending number can't read replies.
Net sales
Everything sold in a period, by every payment method, minus refunds approved in that period.
Estimated profit
Net sales minus what those items cost at today's cost prices. Items with no cost price are left out rather than counted as free.
Coverage
How much of a period's sales the profit figure is based on, e.g. "covers 93% of sales".

Solid arrows are what staff do; dotted arrows are what Sukoon does on its own afterwards.

08:55

Opening: signing in

The owner or cashier opens Sukoon, taps their name and types their password. The Admin (owner) can do everything; a Cashier can sell, look up stock, open Khatas, take payments and request refunds.

On a till's first use, it's asked for a name once ("Till 1"). If WhatsApp sending is paused, an Admin sees a coral banner right away.

09:30

A supplier delivers 24 bottles of oil

The owner opens the product in Stock and records a stock in of 24 with the reason "Delivery". The level goes up and the movement is written into the product's history with the owner's name. From now on, the till won't let anyone sell more bottles than are actually there.

10:00

New items join the catalogue

For a batch of new goods, Bulk entry is the fast screen: scan, type a name and price, press Enter, repeat. For one-offs, New product. Only the name and selling price are required; cost price, category and threshold can wait, and the product is marked needs completing until they're filled.

11:20

A walk-in customer buys oil and atta

The cashier scans the oil: it appears with a − 1 + stepper. They press + once; the cart shows 2. For atta, they choose the product and a weigh box opens: they weigh 1.5 kg on the scale and type it, or type "Rs 200" if the customer asked for Rs 200 worth. The total updates without the page reloading.

The customer pays cash with a Rs 2,000 note; the cashier taps the Rs 2,000 shortcut; Sukoon shows the change in blue. Complete sale saves everything in one go: the sale, its lines, the stock deductions and the invoice number.

11:21

The receipt prints

Sukoon sends the receipt to the thermal printer. If the printer is off or missing, the sale is still complete, and Sukoon offers a PDF receipt instead. After a few seconds the screen returns to an empty till for the next customer.

13:05

A regular puts it on the book

Haji Usman buys Rs 1,770 of goods on credit. The cashier picks Khata, searches "usman", and his name replaces "Walk-in customer". The panel shows his balance after this sale and his limit. If it would take him over his limit, the sale is stopped until the owner types their own name and password into the box on the till.

Once saved, if Haji Usman agreed to WhatsApp updates and his number is confirmed, Sukoon quietly queues a message: "4 items, Rs 1,770, added to your Khata… New balance: Rs 10,030 owed." The message goes out a minute later, and the sale never waits for it.

16:40

Something comes back

A customer returns a dented tin with their receipt. The cashier opens Refunds, types the invoice number, picks the tin, unticks restock (it's damaged) and gives a reason. The refund waits for approval. The owner sees a badge on the Refunds icon, reviews it and approves with their password. Only then does money go back; for a Khata sale, the refund reduces the balance instead of paying cash.

18:15

A Khata payment

Haji Usman pays Rs 5,000 by JazzCash. The cashier opens his Khata, taps Record payment, enters the amount, method and transaction ID. If he pays more than he owes, Sukoon asks first, then keeps the extra as credit. A payment receipt is queued for WhatsApp.

22:00

Closing: how did today go?

The owner opens Insights: today's sales against the same day last week, how much is owed on Khatas, today's estimated profit and margin, the busiest hours, and which categories sold. Reports go deeper and export to CSV (for Excel) or PDF. Low stock becomes tomorrow's order list.

1st, 09:00

Once a month, on its own

On the 1st of each month Sukoon sends WhatsApp statements, with the PDF attached, to customers who agreed. Every day at 11:00 it checks for overdue Khatas and sends a gentle reminder, at most once a week per customer.

The pattern behind the table

Cashiers can do everything that adds to the record: sales, payments, new Khatas, refund requests. The most sensitive of those also need the password typed again, because shop PCs are shared and a signed-in session can be left open.

Part 5

Every feature, explained

Each feature below has four parts: what it is for, how it works on screen, why it was built that way (with the decision record it comes from, like ADR-0014), and the rules it enforces. Decision records are explained in Part 7.

Sign-in and accounts

Who is using Sukoon right now, and what are they allowed to do.

What

Every person has an account with a name, initials, a role (Admin or Cashier) and a password.

How

The sign-in screen shows staff as avatars with initials; tap yours, type your password. You can also type your name or initials. A session lasts up to 10 hours, about a working day.

Why

The design originally showed a 4-digit PIN to switch users quickly. The client chose passwords for everyone instead, because a PIN is easy to watch someone type at a busy counter. ADR-0008

Permissions are a list rather than "if Admin" checks in code, so a new rule is a data change, not a hunt through the program. ADR-0008, 0017

Rules
  • 5 wrong passwords lock that account for 15 minutes. ADR-0017
  • A wrong name and a wrong password give the same message, so the screen can't be used to discover who has an account.
  • Sensitive actions ask for the password again (step-up). ADR-0008
  • Passwords are stored only as a one-way scrambled hash, never as the password itself.

Catalogue: products and categories

The list of everything the shop sells.

What

Products with a name, selling price, optional cost price, category, unit label, whether it's sold loose, and a low-stock threshold. Categories group them.

How

Stock lists every product with its stock bar, price and badges, with search and a "Needs completing" filter. New product is the careful form; Bulk entry is the fast one: scan, type, Enter, and the cursor jumps back to the barcode box. Categories manages groups.

Why

Only name and price are required. Product fields come in three tiers: what a cashier always knows (name, price), what the system fills in (dates, flags), and what's genuinely unknowable at the counter (cost price, category, threshold). The last group may be empty; the product is then "provisional" and shows up as needing completion. ADR-0012

Bulk entry and scan-as-you-go exist because typing in 2,000 products before the first sale would delay opening by weeks. The catalogue can fill up during real trading: if a scanned item isn't found, a cashier can create it on the spot with just name and price. ADR-0011

SKUs are generated from the category's first three letters plus a running number, because the owner wanted shelf codes but nobody wants to invent them. ADR-0018

Rules
  • Only an Admin changes a selling price, with step-up. ADR-0008, 0019
  • A product with any sales or stock history can't be deleted, only deactivated, so history always makes sense.
  • A cashier can create a product at the till but can't edit an existing one.

Barcodes and labels

Getting from a physical item to the right product and price in one scan.

What

Each product can have several barcodes. Any barcode can carry its own price. Goods without a barcode get one generated, printed on sticker sheets.

How

On a product's page: add a barcode (typed or scanned), optionally with a price override and a note; generate an internal SK- barcode; print an A4 PDF sheet of labels on ordinary sticker paper with any office printer.

Why

Many barcodes per product because real goods have them: the same ghee in a new pack design, or a "Near expiry, Oct" sticker that sells cheaper. Stock stays one number, because it's the same ghee. ADR-0009

A4 sticker sheets, not a label printer, because a shop already has an office printer and a dedicated label printer is money and drivers for little gain. ADR-0010

Rules
  • A barcode can belong to only one product in the whole catalogue.
  • An unknown scan gives a calm "not recognised", never a crash, and offers to create the product.
  • Creating a price-override barcode needs the Admin's password (it changes what customers pay).

Stock: levels, movements and alerts

Knowing what's on the shelf, and exactly why the number is what it is.

What

Every product has a stock level that changes only through recorded movements: stock in, stock out, correct count, sale, refund, and found at the till.

How

On a product's page an Admin records a movement with a quantity and a reason. Recent stock movements shows the full history. The Stock screen's tiles show catalogue size, stock value at cost, running low, and out of stock; coloured bars show each product's state.

Why

Every change needs a reason and a name because the audit trail is what makes the numbers trustworthy. If 20 bottles vanish, the history says whether they were sold, written off as broken, or corrected by a count, and by whom.

Sales and losses are different movement types so a month's report can say "Rs 3,000 lost to expiry" instead of hiding it inside sales.

Counted vs never counted. A limit on selling makes sense only once someone knows how many exist. Items created at the till, or bulk-entered and never delivered, sell without a limit and are booked as "found at the till". ADR-0025

Rules
  • Stock can never go below zero, even with two tills selling the last item at the same instant.
  • Sealed packs accept only whole quantities; loose goods accept fractions. ADR-0004
  • "Running low" and "out of stock" are counted separately, so the same item isn't reported twice.

The till: selling

The screen that must never be slow, confusing or wrong.

What

Scan or search items into a cart, adjust quantities or weights, choose a customer and payment method, and complete the sale.

How
  • Sealed packs get a − / + stepper. Loose goods open a weigh box with two modes: Weight (kg) or Amount (Rs), with a live "≈" preview of the other.
  • Rows that still need a weight are amber, and Complete sale stays disabled until every weight is in.
  • Cart changes update just the cart area without reloading the page, so the scan box keeps focus.
  • Cash shows quick-amount buttons and the change. Khata shows a customer search, balance after the sale, the limit, and an approval box if over it.
  • + fades out when the cart already holds all the stock ("That's all of it — 2 bottle in stock").
Why

Weight or amount, because both are how customers actually ask. "Two kilos of atta" and "Rs 200 of atta" are equally common. A USB scale is deferred, with a place left for it in the design. ADR-0005

Rounding once, per line, half up so a refund is always the exact mirror of its sale, and customers see the price they'd expect. ADR-0007

Invoice numbers are claimed in one indivisible step so two tills can never get the same number or leave a gap. ADR-0016, 0021

Each till names itself once in the browser, rather than asking at every login, so sales can be traced to a counter without slowing anyone down. ADR-0023

No discounts in version one. Discounts are one of the commonest ways money leaks from a till; the client chose not to have them yet. ADR-0008, 0020

Rules
  • All-or-nothing: a sale, its lines, its stock deductions, its invoice number and any Khata entry are saved together or not at all. A crash halfway leaves nothing half-saved.
  • Quantities past a counted item's stock are refused, on the server and not just by the faded button.
  • A Khata sale past the limit needs an Admin's own name and password at the till; wrong passwords count toward that account's lockout. ADR-0014, 0026

Receipts

Proof of purchase, that never blocks a sale.

What

After every sale, a receipt: shop name and contact, invoice number, time, cashier, till name, items, total, payment, cash received and change.

How

Sukoon sends it to a thermal receipt printer in that printer's own command language (ESC/POS), over the network, USB or serial. If that fails, the Sale Complete screen offers an 80 mm PDF of the same receipt. Receipts can be reprinted.

Why

The sale is saved first; the receipt comes second. A printer out of paper must never stop a sale or lose a record. That's a general rule in Sukoon: a secondary action never breaks a primary one. ADR-0003

The printed and PDF receipts are built from one shared layout, so they always say the same thing.

Rules
  • Times on receipts are Pakistan time, whatever the PC is set to. ADR-0024
  • Long shop names and addresses wrap to fit 80 mm paper instead of being cut off.

Refunds

Giving money back, safely, with two people involved.

What

Refund part or all of an earlier sale: choose lines and quantities, whether each goes back on the shelf, and a reason.

How

A cashier finds the sale by invoice number and records the refund; it waits as pending approval. Admins see a count badge on the Refunds icon and a "waiting for your approval" card, then approve (with their password) or reject.

Why

Cashier records, owner approves. Fake refunds (pocketing cash against an invented return) are one of the most common ways staff steal from shops. Needing two people removes the opportunity without making every return wait for the owner to type details. ADR-0020

The original invoice is required so a refund can never exceed what was actually sold, and the stock goes back to the right product.

A credit sale's refund reduces the Khata balance instead of paying cash out, because no cash was taken in the first place.

Rules
  • Nothing moves (no cash, no stock, no ledger change) until approval.
  • A pending refund reserves its quantity, so the same goods can't be refunded twice while waiting.
  • A damaged item (restock unticked) is refunded but not put back into stock.

Khata: credit customers

The digital credit book, where the shop's trust meets its money.

What

Customer accounts with a balance, a ledger of every purchase, payment and refund, credit limits and terms, overdue flags, payments by several methods, and statements.

How
  • Khata lists customers with All / Owing / Settled / In credit tabs, overdue and near-limit flags; selecting one shows total purchased, total paid, currently owed, and recent activity.
  • Open a Khata asks for name, phone, how the number was confirmed, and whether they want WhatsApp updates.
  • Record payment: amount, method (cash, bank transfer, JazzCash/Easypaisa, card), optional reference.
  • Statement: pick a month or all time; download an A4 PDF.
Why

Phone numbers are normalised, because "0300 5541298" and "+92 300 5541298" are the same number. Duplicates are flagged, not blocked ("This number is already on Haji Usman's Khata. Same person?"), because family members and shop partners genuinely share phones. ADR-0013

A default limit of Rs 10,000 for new Khatas, so a cashier can't accidentally open unlimited credit. ADR-0026 Over-limit sales need the owner, because the limit exists to be a deliberate decision. ADR-0014

Overdue is measured from the last payment, not per purchase. It answers "has this customer paid anything recently?" without complex allocation of which payment covers which purchase. ADR-0015

Overpayment becomes credit rather than being refused or shown as a confusing minus. Customers are archived, never deleted, because their history is financial record. ADR-0026

One single piece of code writes every ledger entry (sales, refunds and payments all go through it), so the balance and the ledger can never disagree. Tests check they match after every step.

Rules
  • A customer who owes, or is in credit, can't be removed.
  • Changing a confirmed number makes it unconfirmed again, and clears the WhatsApp tick.
  • Statement months follow Pakistan time: a purchase at 1 am on the 1st belongs to the new month.

WhatsApp messages

Automatic, respectful updates that can never slow a sale.

What

Five messages: a credit sale notice, a payment receipt, the monthly statement with its PDF, an overdue reminder, and a neutral account notice for unconfirmed numbers. An Admin Messages screen shows everything.

Al-Rehman General Store: 4 items, Rs 1,770, added to your Khata as of 14 Sep 2026, 8:26 PM. Previous balance: Rs 8,260 owed. New balance: Rs 10,030 owed. This number can't read replies. For questions, contact the shop on 0300 1234567.
How

Nothing is sent during a sale. Each message goes into a queue (an outbox in the database). A background worker sends waiting messages every minute through Meta's official WhatsApp Business API. The Messages screen has the on/off switch, the access key, the reply-to number, the daily limit, a test send, and a log with Retry and Send again.

Status: fully built and tested with a stand-in "fake" sender. The first real send waits for the owner's Meta account (see Part 9).

Why

Meta directly, in the owner's name: official (unofficial WhatsApp tools get numbers banned), no reseller markup, and the owner controls it after the developer hands over. A new SIM is used so the owner keeps their everyday WhatsApp. ADR-0027

Consent first. Only customers who tick "Send updates on WhatsApp" get anything. Money messages go only to confirmed numbers, and permission is re-checked just before sending. ADR-0028

An item count, never item names, because a phone's lock screen is visible to family, and item names are typed by staff. This deliberately differs from the original specification. ADR-0030

Every message says replies aren't read and gives the shop's number, because the shop PC can't receive replies. ADR-0029, 0030

The payment receipt was added after the planning session asked what a customer holds after paying. ADR-0032

Rules
  • Retries: after 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h and 6 h, then "failed". A lost internet connection doesn't use up a try. ADR-0029
  • Age limits: a credit-sale or payment message older than 48 hours is dropped (a stale balance is worse than none); statements and account notices 7 days; reminders 24 hours.
  • If the account itself breaks (expired key, card declined), sending pauses and Admins see a banner; it checks every 30 minutes and resumes by itself.
  • Statements at 9 am on the 1st, caught up until the 8th if the PC was off; reminders checked daily at 11 am, at most one a week, none within 3 days of a statement. ADR-0031
  • A daily limit of 200 messages stops a bug from running up the bill; sending starts off; each message is sent exactly once even if two copies of Sukoon run; a crash mid-send is never automatically resent. ADR-0033
  • The access key is encrypted by Windows and never stored in the database or backups. ADR-0033

Insights and reports

The numbers an owner runs the shop by.

What

An Insights dashboard and five reports: Sales, Estimated profit, Stock value, Low stock, Outstanding credit. Each exports to CSV and PDF.

How

Insights shows today, this week or this month: sales compared with the same point last period, credit outstanding, estimated profit with margin and coverage, busiest hours (or sales by day), sales by category, and recent sales. Reports add custom date ranges and full tables.

Why

Admin only, because totals and margins are owner information. ADR-0034

"Estimated" profit at today's cost prices. Sales don't record what items cost at the time, so profit uses current cost prices and says so. Recording cost at sale would change the database design and was deferred. ADR-0034

Items without a cost price are left out, with coverage stated, because counting them as free would report false profit. ADR-0011, 0034

The screen, the CSV and the PDF are built from one document, so they can never show different numbers.

Rules
  • Weeks run Monday to Sunday; days and months follow Pakistan time.
  • Refunds count in the period they're approved.
  • A comparison with an empty period says "Nothing to compare with", never a percentage from zero.
  • Checked on 10,000 sales: each report computes in under a second.

Shop time

A small feature that touches everything.

What

Every time a person reads (screens, receipts, statements, reports, the year in an invoice number) is shown in one shop timezone, Asia/Karachi by default.

Why

Times are stored in UTC, the world's reference clock, which is correct for storage. But the first version showed UTC directly, so a 6:54 pm sale printed as 13:54. Using a fixed shop setting rather than each PC's own Windows setting means a mis-configured PC can't misprint receipts. ADR-0024

Rules
  • The invoice year changes at Pakistan's midnight, not five hours later.
  • Clock accuracy itself is Windows' job: turn on "Set time automatically" on the shop PC.

Three layers inside the app

The code is organised in layers, with a strict rule about who may talk to whom ADR-0003:

  • Routes receive a click or form from the browser, check permissions, call a service, and return a page. They hold no business rules.
  • Services hold every rule of the business: how a sale is priced, when a refund is allowed, how overdue is worked out. Services never depend on the web framework, so the rules can be tested on their own, thousands of times, in seconds.
  • Models describe the tables in the database: products, sales, customers, ledger entries, messages.

The engineering rules that protect the money

Money and quantities are whole numbers

Computers can't store 0.1 exactly as a decimal fraction, so adding rupees as decimals slowly drifts. Sukoon stores money as whole paisa (Rs 170 → 17000) and quantities as whole thousandths (1.5 kg → 1500). Nothing rounds by accident; rounding happens once, deliberately, per line. ADR-0004, 0007

All or nothing

A sale and everything it changes is saved as one unit, called a transaction. If anything fails partway, none of it is saved: never a sale without its stock deduction, never a payment without its ledger entry.

Safe with two tills at once

When two tills act in the same instant, Sukoon uses single indivisible database steps ("take the next invoice number", "reduce stock only if enough remains", "claim this message only if still waiting"). Tests run dozens of threads at once to prove no number repeats and no stock update is lost.

History is never rewritten

A sale line keeps its own copy of the product's name and price at the time, so renaming or repricing a product later doesn't change old receipts. Things with history are archived, not deleted. Stock only changes through recorded movements.

A secondary action never breaks a primary one

Printing, WhatsApp and similar extras run after the main action is safely saved, and their failures are caught and logged, never shown to the customer as a failed sale. ADR-0003

The database, briefly

The main tables, in plain words: users and permissions; categories, products and barcodes; stock movements; sales and sale items; the invoice counter; refunds and refund items; customers, credit ledger entries and payments; the notification queue with its daily counter and statement runs; and settings. The full design is recorded in one consolidated document ADR-0016. Changes to it are made through migrations: small scripts that upgrade an existing database without losing data, tested both up and down.

How it's tested

  • 497 automated tests, run on every change. Pure tests check rules with no database (pricing, rounding, overdue, message wording); integration tests use a real temporary database and real screens.
  • Hand-calculated scenarios: statements and reports are checked against figures worked out by hand before the code runs.
  • Real browser checks drive Chrome like a person would: clicking +, weighing items, completing sales, watching a WhatsApp message send.
  • Concurrency and performance tests: racing tills, racing message workers, and 10,000 sales.

Part 7

How the project is run

Sukoon is built to a written plan with rules about how decisions are made and recorded. This is what lets a project survive changing developers, a forgotten conversation, or a client asking "why does it do that?" a year later.

The governing documents

  • Development Specification: the technical source of truth: features, phases, required tests, and approval gates.
  • Design System and prototype: how every screen should look and behave. On technical matters the specification wins. ADR-0006
  • Building Software the Right Way: the standards: honest reporting of mistakes, no guessed values, tests alongside features.
  • docs/context.md: the living record: current phase, status, next action, every decision, open questions, known issues, and a session-by-session changelog.
  • docs/glossary.md: every business and technical term as it was defined.

The mechanisms

ADR · Architecture Decision Record
One numbered file per real decision: the context that forced it, the decision, the alternatives considered and why they lost, and the consequences. Written before code. An accepted ADR is binding until a new one replaces it.
grill-with-docs session
A structured interview, started by the human, that attacks a proposal one question at a time before big, hard-to-reverse work (Phase 0's data model, Phase 5's WhatsApp design, Phase 7's packaging). Every answer becomes an ADR.
STOP AND ASK gate
A point in a phase where work must halt until the client approves, recorded in context.md. Used where a mistake would be costly after real data exists.
Definition of Done
The checklist a phase must pass: required tests, verification steps, evidence recorded.

Part 10

Technical words

The software terms used in this handbook, the project documents and our conversations.

Server
The program that holds the data and does the work (Sukoon on the shop PC). Browsers ask it for pages.
Browser / client
What a person looks through: Chrome, Edge, or the Sukoon window.
Route
One address on the server, like /till/ or /khata/12/payment, and the code that handles it.
Service
The part of the code holding business rules, separate from screens.
Model / table
The shape of one kind of stored record (a product, a sale) and where those records are kept.
Database / SQLite
Where all data lives. SQLite keeps it in one file, with a safety mode (WAL) that protects it if the PC loses power mid-write.
Migration
A script that changes the database's structure (e.g. adds the WhatsApp tick) while keeping existing data.
Transaction
A group of database changes saved together or not at all.
Atomic
Done as one indivisible step that nothing else can interrupt halfway.
Concurrency
Two things happening at the same time, like two tills selling the last bottle.
API
A way for one program to use another over the internet; here, Meta's WhatsApp API.
Webhook
When a service calls your server to report something (e.g. "message delivered"). Sukoon doesn't use them, because the shop PC isn't reachable from the internet.
Provider adapter
A thin layer so the WhatsApp company could be swapped without touching business rules.
Queue / outbox
A list of work waiting to be done later, like messages waiting to send.
Background job / worker
Work Sukoon does on its own on a schedule, without anyone clicking.
Dedupe key
A unique label for one event, so the same message can never be queued twice.
Hash
A one-way scramble of a password: the password can be checked, but not recovered.
Session / cookie
How the browser stays signed in, and how a till remembers its name.
DPAPI
Windows' built-in encryption tied to a PC and account, used to protect the WhatsApp key.
UTC
The world's reference time. Pakistan is UTC+5.
ESC/POS
The command language of thermal receipt printers.
CSV
A plain table file that opens in Excel.
htmx / Alpine / Tailwind
Small front-end tools: partial page updates, small interactions, and styling.
PyInstaller / Inno Setup / pywebview
Turn the Python app into a Windows program, an installer for it, and a desktop window around it.
Virtual machine (VM)
A whole computer running inside another, used to build and test the Windows version safely.
Snapshot
A saved state of a VM you can return to, e.g. a spotless Windows for install tests.
Test / test suite
Code that checks other code does what it should; the suite is all of them, run together.
Lint
An automatic check for code style and common mistakes.
Commit / push
Saving a set of changes in the project history, and sending it to GitHub.

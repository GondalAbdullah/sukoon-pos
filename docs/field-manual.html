Part one

The vocabulary

Every confusing word on your screens, in the order you'll meet them. None of this is software jargon — it's how shopkeepers have talked for a hundred years, which is exactly why the app uses it.

At the counter
Till
The checkout counter — historically the cash drawer, now the counter plus the PC sitting on it. A shop can have two or three. Sukoon asks each PC to name itself once (Till 1) so that later, when Rs 400 is missing, you can tell which counter rang the sale. This is why a small "Till 1" chip sits above the scan box, and why it prints on the receipt.
Ring up — a sale
To enter a customer's items and take their money. The whole job of the New sale screen.
Cart
Items scanned so far but not yet paid for. Nothing has left your stock yet and no invoice exists — it's just a scratchpad. It lives in the browser session, so closing the browser mid-sale loses it.
Walk-in customer
Someone who pays now and leaves. No account, no name, no phone number. The overwhelming majority of a general store's sales, which is why it's the default and takes zero clicks.
Cash received (“tendered”)
The physical note the customer hands over. The bill is Rs 1,700; they give you a Rs 2,000 note; cash received is 2,000.
Change
Cash received minus the total — Rs 300 in that example. Sukoon works it out so the cashier doesn't do mental arithmetic with a queue watching.
Exact
A shortcut button meaning "they handed me the precise amount, no change needed." The two buttons beside it are the next round Rs 100 and the next round Rs 500 — because real customers hand over round notes, not exact change. Bill Rs 1,700 → the buttons offer 1,700 (exact), 1,700 (next hundred) and 2,000 (next five hundred).
Invoice number
INV-2026-0001. The permanent receipt number. Never reused, never skipped, even if two tills sell at the same instant — that gap-free sequence is what makes the books auditable.
Subtotal vs Total due
Subtotal is the goods added up. Total due is what they actually owe after discounts. Today they're always identical because discounts aren't built yet — the line is there so the layout doesn't jump when they are.
The goods
Catalogue
The list of everything the shop sells — independent of whether any is on the shelf right now. Cooking Oil stays in the catalogue at zero stock; you haven't stopped selling it, you've just run out.
Sealed pack vs loose
A bottle of oil is a sealed pack — you sell 1, 2, 3, never 1.4. Atta scooped from a sack is loose (or "by weight") — 1.4 kg is perfectly normal. Sukoon refuses fractions on sealed packs and demands a weight on loose goods.
Unit label
What one of this item is: bottle, kg, packet, unit. Purely for reading — "0 bottle" is clearer than "0".
Stock in
Goods arriving — a delivery from the supplier. Raises the level.
Stock out
Goods leaving for any reason other than a sale: breakage, expiry, theft, the owner taking a bottle home. Lowers the level. A sale lowers it too, but is recorded as its own separate type — that's the whole point, so at month end you can see that you lost Rs 3,000 to expiry rather than assuming you sold it.
Stock level
How many are physically on the shelf right now. Sukoon will never let this go below zero.
Low-stock threshold
"Warn me when this drops to 6." Set per product, and optional — no threshold means no warning, which is the right default for a shop with 2,000 lines nobody has time to configure.
Stock value
What the shelf is worth at cost price — what you paid the supplier, not what you charge. That's the number that answers "how much of my money is sitting in this room?"
SKU vs barcode
The SKU (GRO-0001) is your own internal code, generated by Sukoon. The barcode is the manufacturer's printed code on the packet. One product can carry several barcodes — a 1L bottle and its 6-pack carton scan differently but are the same product.
Needs completing
An amber badge meaning: this product was created in a hurry — at the till mid-sale, or through bulk entry — and is missing its cost price, category or threshold. It sells perfectly well. It's a to-do list for the Admin, not an error.
Money, and why it looks odd in the code
Paisa
1 rupee = 100 paisa. Sukoon stores every amount as a whole number of paisa — Rs 170 is stored as 17000. Computers can't hold 0.1 exactly, so storing rupees as decimals makes a shop's books drift by a few rupees a month. Whole paisa can't drift. You never see paisa on screen.
Milli-unit
The same trick for quantity: 1 kg is stored as 1000, so 1.4 kg is 1400. Lets you weigh to the gram with no rounding error.
Credit and refunds
Khata (کھاتا)
The shop's credit book. A trusted regular takes goods now and settles at the end of the month; the shopkeeper writes it in a ledger. In Sukoon, each regular has a Khata with a running balance, and every purchase and payment is a line in it.
Credit limit
The most a customer may owe. Every new Khata starts at Rs 10,000; an Admin can change it. A sale that would go past it is stopped at the till unless an Admin standing there approves it with their own password.
Credit terms & overdue
“Settle within 30 days.” Optional per customer. If they owe money and haven't paid anything in that many days, their Khata is flagged overdue. Any payment, even a small one, restarts the count.
In credit
They paid more than they owed, so the shop owes them. Shown as “Rs 200 in credit” and used up by their next purchase. Sukoon always asks before accepting an overpayment.
Statement
A printable page for one customer and one month (or all time): what they owed at the start, every purchase and payment, what they owe at the end.
Refund
Money back for part or all of a past sale, found by its invoice number. You can refund 1 of the 3 bottles they bought; you don't have to undo the whole sale.
Restock (the tickbox)
Ticked: the item came back sellable and goes onto the shelf again. Unticked: it came back broken or expired — the customer gets their money, the goods go in the bin, and your stock level correctly does not go up.
Pending approval
A cashier records a refund; an Admin approves it. Until approved, nothing moves — no money out, no stock back. This is the single most common way staff steal from a shop, so it's deliberately two people. Pending refunds are waiting at /refunds/pending. There's no link to it in the sidebar — see finding D6.
Refundable
How much of that line is still available to refund: sold, minus already refunded, minus already requested but not yet approved. This is why your screen says "Sold 1.4, Refundable 0.1" — 1.3 is locked up in two pending requests.

Part two

A cashier's day, screen by screen

This is the order things actually happen in a shop — which is also the order to test them in. If you can walk this path without getting stuck, the prototype demos.

1
Sign in

Pick a face, not a username

Shop staff are two or three people who have known each other for years. Making them type sana.khan@ would be absurd, so Sukoon shows their faces and initials; you tap yours and type a short password. Admin can do everything; Cashier can ring sales, look at stock, and request refunds — but not approve them.

2
Stock → New product / Bulk entry

Get the shop into the computer

Before you can sell anything it has to exist in the catalogue. New product is the careful one-at-a-time form. Bulk entry is for the first day of ownership: scan, type a name and a price, press Enter, and the cursor jumps straight back to the barcode box so you can do 400 items without touching the mouse.

Only name and selling price are required. Everything else can be filled later — which is exactly what the amber Needs completing badge tracks.

3
Stock → product → Stock in

Record the delivery

The supplier's van arrives with 24 bottles of oil. You record a stock in of 24 with a reason. The product page's Recent stock movements table is the item's whole life story: every in, every out, every sale, who did it and why. This table is the audit trail — it's the reason the system is trustworthy, and it's worth showing your client explicitly.

4
Till → New sale

Ring up a customer

Scan or type each item. Sealed packs get a − 3 + stepper. Loose goods open a small weight box instead, because the assistant has to walk to the scale and come back — that's why the total shows an amber "Waiting on 1 weight" and the Complete button stays dead until every weight is in.

Then choose Cash, Card or Khata. For cash, tap the round-note button matching what they handed you, read out the change, and hit Complete sale. The receipt goes to the thermal printer; if there's no printer, Sukoon quietly makes a PDF instead rather than failing the sale.

5
Refunds

Take something back

Customer returns with a receipt. Type the invoice number, pick the lines and quantities coming back, decide per line whether it's resellable (restock), and give a reason. The cashier's job ends there — it sits as pending approval until an Admin signs it off on the pending screen.

6
Stock

See what to reorder

End of day, the Admin glances at the four tiles across the top and the coral bars down the list, and writes the supplier order. That's the loop. Everything else in the roadmap — khata, dashboards, reports — hangs off these six steps.

Part three

Ten bugs, all fixed

Each was traced to a specific line, not guessed from a screenshot, then fixed and checked — by an automated test where one can see it, and by driving real Chrome where only a browser can. The rest (B6–B10) turned up while verifying the first five, or while building your two decisions.

B1 Fixed Could lose a sale

A dead “Complete sale” button turns bright green when you hover it

You sawIn one screenshot the button is grey and disabled; in the next, with the same cart, it's dark green and looks perfectly clickable. Nothing about the sale changed — only the mouse position.

WhyThe stylesheet defines the hover colour after the disabled colour, and both are equally specific, so hover wins. The button is still genuinely disabled — clicking does nothing — but it lies about it.

CostA cashier with a queue jabs a button that looks alive and gets no response. That's the single worst thing a till can do.

Wheresukoon/static/css/input.css:58 and :61 — needs an enabled: guard on the hover state.

FixedHover is now guarded with :not(:disabled). A test fails the build if an unguarded button hover ever comes back.

B2 Fixed Visible on every sale

The header says “0 items in the cart” while the cart plainly shows two

You sawTop-left reads 0 items in the cart; the list below reads Cart · 2 items. In the admin screenshot it's 2 against 3. It's always exactly one step behind.

WhyThis one is my fault, and it came from the htmx work. Adding an item now swaps only the #till-body region instead of reloading the page — but that little subtitle sits outside that region, so it never gets replaced. It's showing the count from whenever the page last fully loaded.

Wheretemplates/till/till.html:4 (the subtitle) vs :17 (where the swapped region starts).

FixedEvery cart update now refreshes the top bar too. Checked in Chrome: removing and weighing items keeps both counts in step, with no page reloads.

B3 Fixed Wrong on refunds

Atta sold by the kilo shows up as “1.4 unit” on the refund screen

You sawSold 1.4 unit for a product whose unit is kg, and which says kg correctly everywhere else in the app.

WhyWhen a sale is recorded, Sukoon deliberately freezes a copy of the product's name and price onto the sale line, so that renaming a product later never rewrites history. It just never froze the unit label alongside them — so the refund screen falls back to the generic word "unit".

CostCosmetic, but it's on the screen where someone is handing money back, so it reads as sloppiness.

Wheresukoon/app.py:103 (the fallback) and models/sales.py:63 (no unit is snapshotted).

FixedRefund screens now read the product's real unit — “1.4 kg” — and the pending list shows the product name and who recorded it, instead of “Sale item #12” and “user #2”.

B4 Fixed Cosmetic

The cash shortcut buttons can offer the same amount twice

You sawExact · Rs 3,500 · Rs 3,500 on a Rs 3,470 bill. On an empty cart it's worse: Exact · Rs 0 · Rs 0.

WhyThe buttons compute "round up to the next 100" and "round up to the next 500" independently. For Rs 3,470 both land on Rs 3,500. And on a Rs 1,700 bill, "Exact" and "next 100" are both 1,700.

FixDrop any shortcut that duplicates another, and hide the row entirely on an empty cart.

Wheretemplates/till/till.html, the three quick-amount buttons in the cash panel.

FixedShortcuts are exact, then the next round 100 / 500 / 1,000 / 5,000 — the first three that differ. A Rs 255 bill offers Exact · Rs 300 · Rs 500. The green highlight follows what's actually in the box, and the row hides on an empty cart.

B5 Fixed Misleading number

“Needs attention: 3” and “Out of stock: 3” are the same three items

You sawTwo tiles both reading 3, on a catalogue of four products. It looks like six problems; there are three.

WhyNeeds attention counts running-low plus out-of-stock, so everything in the second tile is also inside the first. The captions make it worse: your three items are fully out, not "running low", and an out-of-stock item needs reordering now, not "soon".

FixCount only genuinely-low items in the first tile, and rewrite both captions to match what's under them.

Whereservices/inventory_service.py:494, captions at templates/stock/list.html:29,34.

Fixed“Needs attention” now counts only running-low items, and the out-of-stock caption reads “reorder now”.

B6 Fixed Worst of the lot

“Change to return” showed Rs 24,500 when the change was Rs 245

FoundNot in your screenshots — every one had cash received equal to the total, so the change box never appeared. It surfaced when the browser check paid Rs 500 for a Rs 255 bill.

WhyThe preview works in paisa (1 rupee = 100 paisa) but printed that number as if it were rupees, so it was 100 times too big. My mistake, from the visual pass; the check I ran then never tried a sale with change.

ReassuranceOnly the preview was wrong. The saved sale, the Sale Complete screen and the receipt all use the server's own figure and were always correct.

FixedChecked end to end: the preview reads Rs 245 and Sale Complete agrees.

B7 Fixed The misalignment you saw

“Cash received” sat flush against the edge of its panel

WhyThree panels asked for padding using a size (px-4.5) the styling tool doesn't have. It doesn't complain; it just produces nothing, so the padding silently vanished. The till's cash panel and two panels on the product page.

FixedReal sizes now, plus a test that scans every template for sizes the tool can't produce.

B8 Fixed Cosmetic

The Refunds icon was a broken squiggle

You sawA stray hook shape in the sidebar and on the Welcome tile, where an undo arrow should be.

FixedThe icon's drawing instructions were corrupted halfway through; restored to the standard undo arrow.

B9 Fixed Silent failures

Error messages at the till never appeared

You'd have seenPressing Set on an empty weight box, or scanning something that didn't match: nothing happened, and no message said why.

WhyThe same mistake as B2, from my htmx work. Updating the cart replaces only the cart area of the page, and the message pop-ups live outside it, so the server's message was handed over and thrown away.

FixedEvery cart update now refreshes the message area too. Checked in Chrome: “Enter a weight.” appears.

B10 Fixed A feature that never worked

An item created at the till could never be sold

WhyScanning something not in the catalogue lets a cashier create it on the spot. But it was created with zero stock, and checkout refuses anything with zero stock, so every such sale failed with “Only 0 unit in stock.” No test had ever tried to finish that sale.

FixedAs part of your stock-limit decision (D5 below): items Sukoon has never counted aren't limited, and the sale records them as “found at the till”. Checked end to end in Chrome.

Part four

Looks wrong. Isn't.

Things in your screenshots that made you stop. D1–D4 are deliberate, and knowing why is what will make the demo land. D5 and D7 were questions for your client; both are now decided and built. D6 was a real gap, now fixed.

D1 By design

Stock value says Rs 0 even though the shelves are full

Stock value is measured at cost price, and none of your four test products has a cost price entered — that's what their Needs completing badges are for. The project's standing rule is to show nothing rather than a plausible-looking guess, so it shows Rs 0 rather than quietly valuing the shelf at retail and overstating the shop's worth.

Enter a cost price on any product and the tile will move. Worth doing before you demo.

D2 By design

“Atta (loose)” appears twice in the same cart

Each scoop is its own weighing. A customer buying 10 kg of atta for the house and 2 kg of a different grade gets two separate lines with two separate weights — so a second scan of a loose product deliberately opens a new weigh-line instead of bumping a counter. Sealed packs do the opposite and increment a stepper, which is why oil shows “3” rather than three rows.

D3 By design

Only 0.1 kg is refundable out of 1.4 kg sold

You have two refunds on that invoice sitting unapproved, for 1.3 kg between them. Pending requests reserve the quantity so the same goods can't be refunded twice while an Admin is still deciding. Approve or reject them at /refunds/pending and the number frees up.

D4 By design

Atta shows 99,997.6 kg in stock

Nothing is wrong — at some point you recorded a stock-in of 100,000 kg while testing. Before any demo, clear the database and seed it with believable numbers; a hundred tonnes of flour in a corner shop will be the only thing your client remembers.

D5 Decided & built

The till stops you before you run out, not at checkout

Your ruleThe moment the cart holds all the stock there is, the + button stops working, with a small amber note: “That's all of it — 2 bottle in stock.” Scanning it again, or typing a weight that's more than what's left, is refused with a message naming the product and how much remains.

Your choiceItems Sukoon has never been told a quantity for (nobody has recorded a delivery or a count) aren't limited, because “0” there means “unknown”, not “none”. At checkout the sold amount is recorded as “Found at the till”, so the stock history stays honest. Record one delivery and the normal limit applies from then on.

Still possibleAnother till selling the last one after it's in your cart. Checkout still catches that, and now says which product.

D6 Fixed

There was no link to the pending-refunds screen

The approval queue worked, but nothing pointed to it, which is why two refunds sat there unnoticed. Now an Admin sees a small count on the Refunds icon in the sidebar, and a “1 refund waiting for your approval → Review” card at the top of the Refunds screen. Cashiers see neither, because they can't approve.

D7 Decided & built

Every time now reads Pakistan time, receipts included

Your choiceOne fixed shop timezone (Asia/Karachi), whatever each PC's Windows setting is. Times are still stored in UTC; only what people read changed: stock history, pending refunds, and both kinds of receipt. The year on invoice numbers now changes at Pakistan's midnight, not five hours later.

One thing only you can doOn the shop PC, turn on Windows' “Set time automatically”. A timezone setting can't fix a clock that's simply wrong.

Before the client sees it

Wipe the test database and reseed. Six to ten products with believable Pakistani names and prices, cost prices filled in, one deliberately low and one deliberately out of stock, and three or four completed sales so the movement history has something in it.

A demo lives or dies on whether the data looks like a real shop. 99,997.6 kg of atta undoes an awful lot of good engineering.
